\chapter{Validation and Final Comparison}
\label{ch:validation}

The previous chapter described the internal pipeline: declaration, lowering,
compilation, contraction and canonicalisation. This chapter records how that
pipeline was checked against an independent reference. The validation is not an
informal visual inspection of printed rules. It is a set of repeatable symbolic
comparisons against FeynRules exports, together with regression tests and
deliberate-corruption tests that establish the comparisons are capable of
failing.

FeynRules is treated throughout as the mathematical oracle. The aim is
therefore not to show that FeynPy and FeynRules produce \emph{identical}
expressions. They do not, and should not be expected to: the two frameworks
differ in how they package charge conjugation, how far they expand covariant
derivatives, and whether chirality is carried by an explicit projector or by
the field class itself. The aim is to show that the two outputs are \emph{equal
as tensors}, that is, that they differ only by index relabelling, reordering of
commuting factors, and the application of gauge and Lorentz identities.

The headline results are that the packaged Standard Model reproduces the
supplied FeynRules export exactly on the full tested interaction set
($163/163$), the unbroken background-field Standard Model reproduces its
reference exactly ($67/67$), and the SMEFT2 Green-basis implementation
reproduces the FeynRules EFT-only \texttt{Ltot} reference on all $184$ exported
rows. Of those $184$ SMEFT2 rows, $161$ agree with no packaging assumption
whatsoever, $15$ agree after one globally fixed charge-conjugation packaging
convention, and $8$ agree after an additional explicitly tabulated
row-specific transformation. No row is accepted through a search over signs or
field orderings, and no row is left unresolved.

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Validation strategy}
\label{sec:validation-strategy}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

The validation is organised in three layers, and it is important not to
conflate them, because the outer two bound what the inner one can mean. A
perfect equality score over a small subset of a model would prove very little.

\paragraph{Model fidelity.} Does the FeynPy source transcribe the operators of
the FeynRules source? This is a question about the input, not about the engine,
and it is answered by direct block-by-block and coefficient-by-coefficient
audit (\Cref{sec:validation-fidelity}).

\paragraph{Vertex equality.} Do the Feynman rules derived from the two
Lagrangians agree as tensor expressions? This is the substance of the
comparison and is answered by canonical tensor-map equality
(\Cref{sec:validation-canonical}).

\paragraph{Coverage.} For how much of the model does the equality test actually
run? A reference export covers a finite set of vertex arities, so some
operators may be unreachable (\Cref{sec:validation-coverage}).

Underneath all three sits an ordinary unit and regression suite covering the
reusable engine: the declaration API, index validation, covariant expansion,
field-strength expansion, field transformations, fermion signs, tensor
canonicalisation and selected benchmark Feynman rules.

A deliberate architectural choice is that model-level comparisons live beside
the concrete model packages under \path{models/}, not inside the generic
contraction engine. The engine computes rules; each model package owns the
conventions and the external-reference adapter needed to compare those rules.
The format-independent comparison machinery itself is shared, and is discussed
in \Cref{sec:validation-generality}.

\section{What ``equal'' means: the canonical form}
\label{sec:validation-canonical}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

Equality is not decided by string comparison, nor by comparing printed
expressions. Both sides are reduced to a canonical tensor-monomial map by
\path{src/symbolic/tensor_canonicalization.py}, and two rules are declared
equal precisely when those maps coincide.

The reduction proceeds in four steps.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Live tensor heads are replaced by temporary heads carrying explicit
    symmetry metadata, so that the canonicaliser can exploit index symmetries
    such as the symmetry of the metric or the antisymmetry of the Levi-Civita
    tensor and the structure constants.
    \item Symbolica's tensor canonicaliser runs with contracted dummy indices
    grouped, which quotients out dummy-index naming and placement.
    \item Dummy names are standardised and commuting factors are reordered into
    a canonical sequence.
    \item The result is read off as a map from canonical tensor monomial to an
    \emph{exact rational} coefficient.
\end{enumerate}

Two properties of this comparison deserve emphasis, because they are what stop
it from degenerating into a tautology.

First, the comparison is performed \emph{separately for each Wilson-coefficient
sector}: both sides are filtered to the terms carrying a given coefficient head
before the maps are compared. A cancellation between different operators
therefore cannot mask an error in either of them. Flavour order and complex
conjugation are retained inside the scalar coefficient, so a row carrying
function-valued coefficients such as $\alpha(f_1,f_2)$ cannot pass vacuously.

Second, only a small and explicitly enumerated set of tensor identities is
used. These are the generator commutator
\begin{equation}
    [T^a, T^b] = i f^{abc} T^c ,
    \label{eq:validation-commutator}
\end{equation}
the structure-constant Jacobi identity
\begin{equation}
    f^{abe}f^{cde} - f^{ace}f^{bde} + f^{ade}f^{bce} = 0 ,
    \label{eq:validation-jacobi}
\end{equation}
and the $SU(2)$ pseudoreality relations that move the antisymmetric
$\epsilon$ tensor through a weak generator. These are the identities that let a
non-identical but mathematically equal expression be recognised as equal.

Just as importantly, the comparison does \emph{not} use equations of motion,
integration by parts, momentum conservation, Schouten identities, Fierz
identities, or any broad pattern-matching simplification. Admitting those would
make the test far weaker, because an incorrect rule could then often be
massaged into the reference.

Raw coefficient-head occurrence counts are also recorded, but they are
diagnostics rather than an equality criterion. Two equivalent expanded forms
can legitimately have different raw counts before canonicalisation: for
instance, FeynPy prints the two antisymmetric branches of
$\mathrm{Dual}[F] = \tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon \cdot F$ separately, whereas FeynRules
has already collapsed them using $\epsilon$ antisymmetry. On the current SMEFT2
reference, $100$ of $182$ shared signatures have matching raw counts, and every
one of the remaining deltas carries a recorded benign reason. None is
unexplained.

charge-conjugation packaging transform derived in \Cref{sec:validation-ec}. A

signature, phase and duplicate-leg symmetry are all fixed in advance of the

state of the comparison reported $176$ direct matches, because the rows that
depend on the global \texttt{Ec} convention were counted as though they needed
nothing. That figure was not wrong about equality, but it was misleading about
assumptions: it presented as unconditional a set of rows whose agreement rests
on a convention. The current grading separates the two, so that the strength of
the claim is visible in the numbers themselves.

The bosonic canonical tensor-map figure of $32/32$ refers specifically to the
bosonic sector diagnostic and should not be read as a coverage ceiling. The
remaining $152$ fermionic rows are proved through the same canonical-map
machinery, reported in the exact-symbolic column.

\section{The evanescent charge-conjugation convention}
\label{sec:validation-ec}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

The single most important assumption in the fermionic sector concerns the
evanescent charge-conjugated four-fermion operators, that is, every coefficient
whose name begins with \texttt{alphaEc}. It is documented here in full, because
it is load-bearing: without it, all $21$ four-fermion \texttt{Ec} rows fail.

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The discrepancy}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

FeynRules writes these operators through the \texttt{CC[...]} macro. Taking the
four-lepton operator as the representative case,
\begin{verbatim}
alphaEcll[f1,f2,f3,f4] CC[LLbar[sp1,ii,f1]].LL[sp1,jj,f2]
                       .LLbar[sp2,jj,f3].CC[LL[sp2,ii,f4]]
\end{verbatim}
The \texttt{ExpandIndices} step resolves the conjugation, so the exported
vertex carries \emph{no residual charge-conjugation matrix}: the spinor flow
runs from external leg $1$ to leg $4$ and from leg $2$ to leg $3$ through
ordinary spinor-metric and gamma chains.

The FeynPy source instead keeps the charge-conjugation matrices explicit and
pairs adjacent legs,
\begin{equation}
    \bar{l}_L(s_1)\, C(s_1,s_2)\, l_L(s_2)\;
    \bar{l}_L(s_3)\, C(s_3,s_4)\, l_L(s_4) ,
    \label{eq:validation-ec-feynpy}
\end{equation}
that is, flow $(1,2)$ and $(3,4)$ with two explicit $C$ factors.

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The transform}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

The two forms describe the same operator. Mapping the FeynPy packaging onto the
FeynRules packaging eliminates both $C$ factors by re-pairing the four spinor
arms in \emph{crossed} order, $(\text{arm } 0, \text{arm } 3)$ and
$(\text{arm } 1, \text{arm } 2)$, and picks up a single overall sign from the
antisymmetry of the charge-conjugation matrix,
\begin{equation}
    C^{T} = -C ,
    \label{eq:validation-c-antisymmetry}
\end{equation}
together with the anticommutation required to reorder the fermion fields into
the crossed pairing.

Both the pairing and the sign are global constants, fixed once for every
\texttt{alphaEc} head rather than chosen per row. In the implementation they
appear as two named constants, \texttt{\_EC\_CC\_CONVENTION\_MODE} set to
\texttt{crossed} and \texttt{\_EC\_CC\_CONVENTION\_PHASE} set to $-1$.

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Why this is a derivation and not a fit}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

A reader is entitled to ask how a convention that rescues an entire sector can
be distinguished from a parameter tuned until the answer came out right. The
answer is that the choice is heavily overdetermined by the data. There are only
four possible combinations of pairing mode and overall sign, and exactly one of
them reproduces the reference, uniformly across the sector, as shown in
\Cref{tab:validation-ec-overdetermination}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
Pairing mode & Phase & Rows reproduced (of 21) \\
\midrule
crossed & $-1$ & 15 \\
crossed & $+1$ & 0 \\
direct  & $-1$ & 3 \\
direct  & $+1$ & 2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Only one of the four possible conventions reproduces the FeynRules
reference. The $15$ rows in the accepted row are completed by the pinned
transforms of \Cref{sec:validation-ec-pinned}; the small counts in the
rejected rows are rows on which the transform does not apply at all.}
\label{tab:validation-ec-overdetermination}
\end{table}

The contrast is stark rather than marginal: the three alternatives fail on
essentially every row. A per-row fit would have $4^{21}$ degrees of freedom,
whereas what is actually used is a single global choice out of four, three of
which are excluded by the data. Removing the transform altogether causes all
$21$ rows to fail, which is what establishes that it is doing real work and
must therefore be stated rather than left implicit.

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Residual row-specific packaging}
\label{sec:validation-ec-pinned}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

Eight rows require a further, row-specific charge-conjugation transform on top
of the global convention.

Two are Weinberg-operator rows. FeynRules packages them as same-chirality
charge-conjugated lepton structures, whereas FeynPy stores the mixed
$\bar{l}_L / l_L$ form with the charge-conjugation tensor explicit. The
accepted transform compares the FeynRules row against the antisymmetrised local
pair $\mathrm{FeynPy}(\bar{l}_L, l_L) - \mathrm{FeynPy}(l_L, \bar{l}_L)$, and
the relative minus sign is derived from \Cref{eq:validation-c-antisymmetry}
rather than searched.

The remaining six rows, covering $12$ coefficient sectors, are evanescent
\texttt{alphaEc*} partner rows. These use an explicit rule table in which each
entry fixes exactly one partner signature, one phase, one duplicate-leg
symmetry mode, and a provenance string naming the source operator. Nothing is
searched at acceptance time. Dedicated sidecar checks confirm the Weinberg
comparison at $2/2$ and the evanescent charge-conjugation checks at $12/12$,
and both sidecars also verify that the \emph{wrong} sign or the wrong
combination does \emph{not} match, which is what makes them evidence rather
than restatement.

\section{Chirality}
\label{sec:validation-chirality}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

The SMEFT2 parser discards the \texttt{ProjM} and \texttt{ProjP} chirality
projectors that FeynRules prints, on the grounds that in the unbroken basis
every matter field is already chiral and the projector is therefore redundant
with the field itself. Redundancy of this kind is a reasonable expectation, but
it is not something to assume silently: if a projector disagreed with the field
it sits between, dropping it would hide a genuine discrepancy. The agreement is
therefore verified rather than assumed.

The rule that makes the projector predictable is elementary. Conjugation flips
chirality, so a left-handed field has a right-handed conjugate,
\begin{equation}
    \overline{P_X \psi} = \bar\psi\, P_{\bar X} ,
    \label{eq:validation-conjugate-chirality}
\end{equation}
where $\bar X$ denotes the chirality opposite to $X$. Each gamma matrix
anticommutes with $\gamma_5$ and therefore commutes a projector into its
opposite, $\gamma^\mu P_X = P_{\bar X} \gamma^\mu$. For a bilinear
$\bar\psi_X \Gamma^{(n)} \psi_Y$ carrying $n$ gamma matrices, pushing the left
projector rightwards through the chain gives
\begin{equation}
    \bar\psi_X \, \Gamma^{(n)} \, \psi_Y
    = \bar\psi \, \Gamma^{(n)}
    \begin{cases}
        P_{\bar X} P_Y \, \psi , & n \text{ even},\\
        P_{X} P_Y \, \psi , & n \text{ odd}.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:validation-chirality}
\end{equation}
Since $P_A P_B = \delta_{AB} P_B$, the chain is non-vanishing only when
$Y \neq X$ for even $n$ and $Y = X$ for odd $n$, and whenever it survives the
single remaining projector is $P_Y$. That is the prediction.

A chain whose two endpoints have the same ``barredness'' runs through a
charge-conjugation matrix instead of the spinor metric. Because $C$ itself
commutes chirality, this flips the parity condition on $n$, and for a
barred/barred chain it also flips which of the two chiralities survives. Both
cases are handled explicitly.

This rule predicts every projector in the reference export with no exceptions:
all $2998$ of them, comprising $2806$ ordinary chains and $192$
charge-conjugation chains. Any projector contradicting the rule, or any chain
that vanishes identically for chirality reasons, raises an error and the row is
reported as failed rather than matched. The check is model independent and lives
in the shared comparison layer, so other models can reuse it.

\section{Mutation sensitivity}
\label{sec:validation-sensitivity}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

Every statement above concerns comparisons that \emph{succeeded}. On its own
that is weak evidence, because a comparison that had silently degenerated into
a tautology would also report a perfect score. The suites therefore include
deliberate-corruption tests which inject a physically meaningful error into the
reference and assert that the comparison notices.

For SMEFT2 the injected corruptions are a rescaled Wilson coefficient in both
the two-fermion and four-fermion sectors, a global sign flip, chirality flips
in both directions, transposed flavour slots, and a removed imaginary unit.
None is reported as agreement. The suite also asserts that the unmutated rows
\emph{are} accepted, so it cannot pass vacuously by failing everything.

The shared machinery carries an analogous suite under \path{models/SM/}, with
six corruptions: a chirality flip, a wrongly conjugated CKM element, a reversed
colour generator, a removed imaginary unit, a wrong derivative momentum, and a
global sign flip. All six are detected.

Two further structural facts support the same conclusion. No coefficient head
is exempted from comparison: the exemption set is empty. And the exception
tables are small and enumerated rather than open-ended. The raw head-count
deltas are explained by only four reason categories
(\texttt{DUAL\_FS\_ANTISYMMETRY}, \texttt{DUMMY\_LORENTZ\_MERGE},
\texttt{EXACT\_SYMBOLIC\_CANONICAL\_EQUIVALENCE} and
\texttt{PINNED\_CC\_CANONICAL\_EQUIVALENCE}), and the packaging exceptions
consist of exactly $12$ pinned rules, each carrying a recorded provenance
string naming its source operator.

\section{Coverage and limits}
\label{sec:validation-coverage}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

Of the $303$ parameters declared in the SMEFT2 model, $295$ appear in at least
one validated reference vertex. The eight that do not divide into two groups.

Five are Standard Model parameters, $\lambda$, $\mu_H$ and the three Yukawa
matrices, which are correctly absent from an EFT-only \texttt{Ltot}.

The remaining three are genuine EFT coefficients that the comparison does not
reach: \texttt{alphaKB}, \texttt{alphaR2B} and \texttt{alphaOmuH2}. The reason
is structural rather than accidental. The reference export covers vertices of
arity three to six, whereas these three coefficients multiply operators that
generate only two-point vertices: two are built from the abelian field
strength, whose non-abelian self-interactions are absent, and the third is the
Higgs mass term. They are therefore unreachable by this reference, and are
reported as unvalidated rather than counted as covered. A regression test pins
this list so that the gap cannot widen unnoticed.

This is the complete coverage gap. Every other coefficient that contributes to
a two-point vertex, including the gauge and Higgs kinetic normalisations and
the fermion kinetic terms, also appears in validated vertices of three or more
legs, where gauge invariance relates the two-point normalisation to the
higher-point ones. Closing the remaining gap would require exporting two-point
vertices from FeynRules, which is a straightforward extension of the reference
rather than a change to the comparison.

Two further limits should be stated plainly. The comparison is tree-level and
concerns vertex tensor structure only; it says nothing about parameter-card
semantics, loop-level output or external export formats. And in the Standard
Model benchmark the gauge-fixing sector is excluded, as noted in
\Cref{sec:validation-sm}.

\label{sec:validation-generality}

Applying the machinery to a further model requires four things: a parser

Models with Wilson coefficients additionally need coefficient-head filtering.
The machinery is therefore general in substance, though not plug-and-play.

SMEFT2 retains its own parser because its export uses syntax the Standard Model
sector parsers do not cover: indexed Wilson coefficients, slashed momenta
inside spinor chains, two-index weak $\epsilon$ tensors, and generic index
deltas resolved by index prefix. Everything genuinely model independent is
shared rather than duplicated, including the bracket and comma scanning, the
FeynRules label normalisation, the bosonic comparator, the canonical
coefficient maps, and the chirality validator of
\Cref{sec:validation-chirality}.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:validation-reproducibility}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

From the repository root, the full regression suite is run with
\begin{minted}{bash}
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q
\end{minted}
On the final checked state this reports $519$ passing tests.

The accepted SMEFT2 gate is
\begin{minted}{bash}
.venv/bin/python -m models.SMEFT2.comparison --check --allow-cc-packaging
\end{minted}
This accepts the documented global \texttt{Ec} convention and the pinned
packaging rows, and fails on any unresolved packaging row, operator-content
miss, symbolic inequality, symbolic error, or unexplained FeynPy-only
signature.

The stricter audit gate omits the flag,
\begin{minted}{bash}
.venv/bin/python -m models.SMEFT2.comparison --check
\end{minted}
and requires every row to be a direct match with no packaging assumption at
all. It therefore fails on the current state, by construction, because $23$
rows are documented packaging matches. Its purpose is to detect whether that
set has changed, and it is not the acceptance criterion used here.

The mutation-sensitivity suite of \Cref{sec:validation-sensitivity} is run on
its own with
\begin{minted}{bash}
.venv/bin/python -m pytest \
  models/SMEFT2/tests/test_smeft2_comparison_sensitivity.py -q
\end{minted}

The human-readable report is regenerated with
\begin{minted}{bash}
.venv/bin/python -m models.SMEFT2.comparison
\end{minted}

The main reproducibility artifacts are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \path{models/SMEFT2/VALIDATION_ANALYSIS.md}, the maintained analysis
    of what the comparison proves and where its assumptions lie;
    \item \path{models/SMEFT2/COMPARISON.md}, the generated human-readable
    summary;
    \item \path{models/SMEFT2/COMPARISON_METHOD.md}, the maintained method
    report;
    \item \path{models/SMEFT2/comparison/artifacts/vertex_comparison_report.json},
    the per-signature comparison rows, from which
    \Cref{app:smeft2-rows} is generated;
    \item \path{models/SMEFT2/comparison/artifacts/feynpy_vertices.json}, the
    regenerated local FeynPy vertex rules.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:validation-summary}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Established.} The FeynPy SMEFT2 Feynman rules agree with FeynRules
as tensor expressions, sector by sector in the Wilson coefficients, for all
$184$ exported vertices of arity three to six, covering $295$ of $303$ model
parameters. Agreement is proved by exact rational equality of canonical
tensor-monomial maps, is demonstrably sensitive to injected errors, and rests
on no unenumerated exceptions. The Standard Model and unbroken background-field
benchmarks match at $163/163$ and $67/67$.

\paragraph{Assumed, and documented.} One global charge-conjugation packaging
convention relating the FeynRules \texttt{CC[...]} flow to the explicit $C$
factors of the FeynPy source, plus $12$ pinned row-specific packaging rules.
These affect $23$ of $184$ rows and are reported under distinct statuses rather
than folded into the direct-match count.

\paragraph{Outside scope.} The two-point sector, hence \texttt{alphaKB},
\texttt{alphaR2B} and \texttt{alphaOmuH2}; the Standard Model gauge-fixing
sector; and everything beyond tree-level vertex tensor structure.
